Every hard-tech pitch claims a moat. Usually the claim is a patent and a good demo. When I dig into a company’s technicals, I look for something underneath that holds.

The questions I ask

  • Where is the hard part? A clever design can be reverse-engineered. A manufacturing process that took years to get right is much harder to copy.
  • Could a strong team rebuild this from the paper in a year? If yes, the company has a head start and little else.
  • Does it get better with every run? Data and know-how that build up with each unit shipped or experiment run are a real advantage.
  • Who controls the supply chain? A single critical supplier can be a risk or a moat, depending on who holds the relationship.
  • Where does the value sit? Components get commoditised. Depth shows in how well the mechanical, chemical, electronic and control layers work together.

Answers that make me take it seriously

The best answers are specific and a little boring: a process step only this team can run, a dataset nobody else has, a supplier relationship built over years. Big claims with no detail behind them are the warning sign.

Why I care: I have done engineering, procurement and techno-economic work on real systems, so I know where the hard parts usually hide.
