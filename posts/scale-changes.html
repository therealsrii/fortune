I have worked at both ends of the scale, from microfluidic channels where the depth ratio matters to systems that move hundreds of litres a minute. The biggest lesson is that scaling up changes the problem itself.

The physics changes

At the microscale, surface tension and viscosity dominate. Flow is smooth and predictable, and two streams can run side by side without mixing.

At industrial scale, those advantages disappear. You are dealing with mixing, wear, pumping energy, corrosion and the cost of materials by the tonne. A process that worked well on a chip can be impossible in a plant.

The gap in the deck

Most pitch decks show a lab result on one slide and a revenue chart on the next. The scale-up sits between those two slides, and it decides whether the company survives.

What I look for

  • Has the team run anything at an intermediate scale?
  • Do they know which physical effect stops working when they scale, and what replaces it?
  • Are equipment, materials and procurement costed at full-scale prices?

Why I care: I work on fluid systems from microlitres to hundreds of litres per minute.
