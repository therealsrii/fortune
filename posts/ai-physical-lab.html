Generative AI has spread fast through text, code and images. The physical lab is lagging behind. The common assumption is that robots will close the gap, with arms and humanoids doing what a person at the bench does.

As an engineer who builds lab hardware, I see a bigger opportunity in AI in science itself.

Experiments set the pace

A lab moves as fast as the number of experiments it can run, how quickly it learns from them, and how well it chooses the next one.

AI fits here. Models can look at results and help decide what to test next, while the hardware runs many conditions at once.

Multiplexing is the lever

A multiplexed system runs hundreds of conditions in parallel on a single device. Microfluidics is one way to get there, with many experiments on one chip at tiny volumes.

Pair that hardware with a model that helps steer it, and the whole workflow could change. That is the part I’m exploring now.

What I’d want to see

  • Hardware designed for parallel experiments from the start.
  • A model that helps choose the next experiment.
  • A clear answer to one question: how many more questions a month can the team answer?

Why I care: I fabricate microfluidic devices and photo-actuated chips, the physical side of this work. I’m still learning the modelling side.
