Engineers love the technology. It is easy to spend two years making a process faster, purer or more elegant, then discover that the number you optimised never decided whether the business works.

An early techno-economic analysis (TEA) prevents that.

What a TEA tells you

A good TEA shows the few parameters that drive the cost and value of a product. It might be yield, energy, a reagent, or how long equipment sits idle. Once you know, you know where to spend your time.

It also shows when a clever process will never pay for itself.

Context changes the answer

The same product can have very different economics depending on where and how it is made and used. Climate, soil, local supply and the choice of manufacturing method can each move the cost of everything downstream.

What I look for

  • Does the team know its top three cost drivers without looking them up?
  • Is the TEA built from real quotes and measured performance?
  • Has it changed what the team works on?

Why I care: I have written technical reports and TEAs for companies in biomanufacturing and agriculture, and I work on engineering and procurement in critical minerals.
