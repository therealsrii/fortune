I'm Sri, a mechanical engineer by trade and a PhD candidate in the School of Biomedical Engineering. My research sits at the intersection of semiconductors, biology and fluid dynamics, aimed at critical and pressing problems in healthcare.

I work on fluid flow across eight orders of magnitude, from microfluidic probes running at microlitres per minute to systems moving hundreds of litres per minute, and I can tell what will hold up at each scale. The work runs across industries too, from healthcare to critical minerals, agriculture and biomanufacturing, and the question is usually the same: does the science still work at scale, and at a price anyone will pay?

Hardware and biological research still move in cycles of months and years. Shortening those cycles is the work that matters most to me, and lately it has pulled me toward AI. I'm a scale-up engineer exploring where AI can make that work faster: choosing which experiments to run, running more of them in parallel, and catching problems before anything gets built. One idea I keep coming back to is biological data on request, where you ask a specific question and an automated lab sends back the data.
